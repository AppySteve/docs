Integrations let your agents work inside your existing stack — reading data, taking actions, and interacting with other platforms on your behalf. Connect once and your agents handle the rest.

Connect with over 2,000 applications — including HubSpot, Slack, Salesforce, Google Docs, Google Calendar, Gmail, Google Drive, Google Sheets, Jira, GitHub, Linear, Shopify, Stripe, Intercom, and more. For each integration, your agents get access to specific actions: reading emails, creating calendar events, updating records, sending messages.

When a task requires an integration, your agent walks you through connecting your account. Authorize the service once, and your agents can work with it from then on. Disconnect any integration at any time.

Your agents never see or store your passwords. Each operates within defined, scoped permissions — no blanket access.

## Connecting a service that isn't in the catalog

If a tool you use isn't in the 2,000+ app catalog, just tell Violet what you want to connect and what you need it to do. Violet researches the service's API and builds the connection herself — you never touch a specification, a schema, or any technical setup.

Once she's built it, she sends you one link to authorize the service. From there it works like any other integration: your agent reads data, takes actions, and asks you to confirm the first time it does anything that changes data.

Two ways this can go, depending on how the service exposes itself:

- **API key or token.** Violet defines the connection from the service's API docs and sends you a secure page to enter your credentials. She never asks for a key, password, or account ID in chat.
- **Sign in with your account (OAuth).** Some services — Day.ai is one — only expose their capabilities through a remote MCP server behind your own sign-in page. For these, you authorize by signing in on the service's own page, the same way you would connect any app; Violet never sees or holds a credential for it.

<Info>
  Tell Violet whether the connection should only read data or also make changes — she asks up front, and it shapes what the connection is allowed to do. Adding capability later is just asking again; the connection doesn't need to be rebuilt from scratch.
</Info>

### Who can see and use it

Once a custom integration is connected, it's visible to everyone in your organization — not just the person who built it. Any teammate can ask their agent to use it, the same as a catalog integration.

The connection you authorize is still yours: your credentials stay tied to you, and a teammate using the integration doesn't see or borrow them. If the team should share one connection instead of everyone connecting individually, that's a deliberate step — ask Violet to make it a shared, organization-wide connection.

Only the person who originally built the integration can change what it does. Everyone else can use it as-is.
